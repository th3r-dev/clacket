What you get

Thirteen recorded switches

Linear, tactile and clicky, from a soft NK Cream to a buckling spring. Space, Enter and Backspace get their own recordings where the switch has them, plus a quiet sound when you lift the key.

A sound for any key

Click a key on the on-screen keyboard, or just press it, and give it any switch's sound or your own MP3 or WAV. The drawing follows your keyboard's shape, ANSI, ISO or JIS, and shows your own language's letters.

Mouse, trackpad and plugs

Clicks, scroll ticks, taps, swipes and pinches. A sound when you plug in a USB drive, a phone, a charger, a display or headphones, with the port when your system reports it.

It never phones home

Clacket listens to your keys only to play a sound. It doesn't record what you type, keep it, or send anything anywhere. There is no account, no analytics and no update check. The build fails if networking code gets into the app.

First launch

macOS: Clacket asks for Input Monitoring so it can hear your keys. Turn it on in System Settings → Privacy & Security → Input Monitoring, then reopen Clacket. If macOS says the developer can't be verified, right-click Clacket in Applications and choose Open.

Linux: add yourself to the input group, then log out and back in.
